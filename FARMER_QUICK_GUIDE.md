# Crop Health & Marketplace - Quick Guide for Farmers

## 🌾 How to Use Crop Health Module

### Step 1: Access Crop Health Dashboard
1. Login to your (J)ai Kisan account
2. Click **"🌾 Crop Health"** in the top menu
3. You'll see 4 main options:
   - 📸 **Photo Doctor** - Take photo of problem
   - 🛡️ **IPM Advisor** - Get 3-step treatment plan
   - 🚨 **Report Pest** - Alert nearby farmers
   - 🏪 **Marketplace** - Find shops & prices

### Step 2: Know Your Crop's Enemies
1. On Crop Health dashboard, select your crop from dropdown
2. See all major pests and diseases for that crop
3. Check symptoms to identify what's attacking your crop
4. Note the ETL (Economic Threshold Level) - when to spray

### Step 3: Get IPM Recommendations
1. Click **"IPM Advisor"**
2. Select your crop
3. Enter the pest/disease name (e.g., "Pink Bollworm")
4. Enter how bad it is (e.g., "10 per plant" or "20% affected")
5. Click **"Get IPM Recommendations"**

**You'll get 3-tier advice:**
- **Tier 1:** Prevention methods (use before pest appears)
- **Tier 2:** Organic & mechanical methods (try first when pest appears)
- **Tier 3:** Chemical methods (only if Tier 2 fails and ETL is crossed)

### Step 4: Find & Buy Products
1. Click **"Marketplace"**
2. Enter product name (e.g., "Neem Oil", "Sticky Traps")
3. See list of shops in your area with phone numbers
4. Compare prices from different brands
5. Look for **"Kifayati"** tag - these are cheapest options
6. Call shop to confirm stock before visiting

### Step 5: Report Pest Outbreak
1. Click **"Report Pest"**
2. Fill the form:
   - Select crop
   - Enter pest/disease name
   - Select severity (Low/Medium/High/Critical)
   - Describe symptoms
3. Click **"Submit Report"**

**Why Report?**
- When 3+ farmers report same pest, everyone nearby gets automatic alert
- Helps community take preventive action early
- You're helping your fellow farmers!

## 🛡️ 3-Step IPM Strategy (Easy to Remember)

### Step 1: PREVENT (Bhoomi Raksha)
**Do BEFORE pest appears:**
- ✓ Treat seeds with Trichoderma (₹50-100/acre)
- ✓ Do deep ploughing in summer (₹800-1200/acre)
- ✓ Rotate crops smartly (tomato ≠ after potato)
- ✓ Clean field after harvest (₹500-800/acre)
- ✓ Use balanced fertilizer (avoid excess nitrogen)

**Saves:** 60-70% pest problems

### Step 2: ORGANIC (Jugaad)
**Do WHEN pest appears:**

**For Flying Insects (Whitefly, Aphid):**
- ✓ Yellow sticky traps (₹150-250/acre)
- ✓ Neem oil spray (₹400-600/acre)

**For Caterpillars/Borers:**
- ✓ Bird perches (₹100-200/acre)
- ✓ Pheromone traps (₹800-1200/acre)
- ✓ Garlic-chili-ginger spray (₹100-150/acre)

**For All Pests:**
- ✓ Light traps for night-flying moths
- ✓ Dashparni Arka (10-leaf extract)

**Effectiveness:** 60-80% control

### Step 3: CHEMICAL (Last Resort)
**Do ONLY when:**
- ✓ Tier 2 failed
- ✓ Pest count above ETL (check IPM Advisor)
- ✓ Crop damage reaching 30%+

**REMEMBER:**
- ⚠️ **Check ETL first** - never spray below threshold
- ⚠️ **Wear mask, gloves, full clothes**
- ⚠️ **Spray only morning (6-9 AM) or evening (4-6 PM)**
- ⚠️ **NO spray if rain in 4 hours**
- ⚠️ **NO spray during flowering**
- ⚠️ **Wait PHI days before harvest**

## ⚠️ Banned Chemicals - DO NOT USE!

### NEVER Use These (Banned):
1. **Monocrotophos** - Extremely poisonous
2. **Phorate** - Contaminates groundwater
3. **Carbofuran** - Kills birds, very toxic
4. **Methyl Parathion** - Extremely toxic
5. **DDT** - Damages soil permanently
6. **Endosulfan** - Poisons water bodies

**What to Do:** Use the "Check Chemical Ban" feature in IPM Advisor

## 🏪 How to Save Money (Kifayati Shopping)

### Rule 1: Compare Before Buying
- Same chemical, different brands have different prices
- Example: Imidacloprid
  - Bayer Confidor: ₹450
  - Dhanuka/UPL: ₹350-380 ⭐ **Save ₹100!**

### Rule 2: Try Cooperative Societies
- Often 10-20% cheaper than private dealers
- Example: Kisan Sewa Kendra, Krishi Seva stores
- Look for "Kifayati prices" in Marketplace

### Rule 3: Use Organic First
- Neem oil: ₹380-450/liter (lasts 2-3 sprays)
- Yellow sticky traps: ₹150-250/acre (reusable 2-3 weeks)
- Often cheaper than chemicals in long run

### Rule 4: Buy Only What You Need
- Check ETL before buying chemicals
- Don't stock pile (chemicals expire)
- Buy from nearby shops (save transport cost)

## 📱 Quick Actions

### Morning Routine (6:00 AM)
1. Check Crop Health dashboard for alerts
2. If alert for your crop → Read preventive measures
3. If planning to spray → Check weather (rain forecast?)
4. Best time to spray: 6-9 AM

### Evening Routine (4:00 PM)
1. Walk your field, check for pest symptoms
2. If new problem → Take photo, use Photo Doctor
3. If identified pest → Check ETL, get IPM recommendation
4. If need product → Find shops in Marketplace
5. Second-best time to spray: 4-6 PM

### Weekly Task (Sunday)
1. Check "Neighborhood Watch" for pest reports
2. If neighbors reporting pest in your crop → Take preventive action
3. If you see pest → Report it (help others)

## 🚨 Emergency: Severe Pest Attack!

### If 50%+ Crop Affected:
1. **DON'T PANIC** - Spraying wrong chemical can make it worse
2. Go to **IPM Advisor** immediately
3. Select crop and pest name
4. Check if pest count is above ETL (if yes, chemical may be needed)
5. Get Tier 2 organic remedies first
6. If Tier 2 not enough → Check Tier 3 chemicals
7. Buy from nearest shop (use Marketplace)
8. **READ SAFETY INSTRUCTIONS CAREFULLY**
9. Spray in evening (4-6 PM) with proper gear
10. Report the outbreak to help neighbors

## 💡 Pro Tips

### Tip 1: Prevention is Cheapest
- ₹100-200 on prevention saves ₹1000+ on chemicals later
- Healthy soil = Strong plants = Fewer pests

### Tip 2: Never Spray "Just in Case"
- Kills beneficial insects (ladybirds, spiders, bees)
- Wastes money
- Pollutes environment
- **Check ETL first!**

### Tip 3: Rotate with Organic
- Mix 1 chemical spray + 2 organic sprays
- Reduces chemical use by 60%
- Saves money, protects soil

### Tip 4: Use Farmer Friends
- Ladybird beetles eat aphids
- Spiders eat many pests
- Dragonflies control mosquitoes
- Bees are essential for pollination
- **Protect them - they're free pest control!**

### Tip 5: Keep Records
- Date of spraying
- Chemical name and dose
- Result (effective or not)
- Cost spent
- Helps decide what works best for your field
